# **jazz vs. hip hop: comparing the cultural influence of these transformative musical genres**

## **jazz in the 1920s**

### *context*
- **who**: jazz was created by african american musicians like louis armstrong, duke ellington, and bessie smith
- **what**: its a music style that mixes african and european musical elements, with a lot of improvisation
- **when**: jazz became popular in the 1920s, a time of big changes in technology and society
- **where**: it started in new orleans and spread to other big cities like chicago, new york, and kansas city
- **why**: jazz was a way for african americans to express themselves

### *important vocabulary and historical terms*
- **harlem renaissance**: a cultural movement in the 1920s, mainly in harlem, new york, that celebrated african american art, music, and literature
- **flapper**: young women in the 1920s who challenged traditional gender roles by wearing short dresses, dancing freely, and being more independent

### *impact on people and country*
- **social liberation**: jazz affected society in many ways, like fashion and industry. it also showed new ideas about race, gender, and youth
- **racial integration**: jazz brought together people from different races, helping break  segregation in clubs and dance halls
- **economic influence**: jazzs popularity helped create many clubs and speakeasies, contributing to the economy and nightlifr culture

### *lessons for today*
- **cultural integration**: jazz teaches us how art can bring people together across racial and cultural lines
- **artistic freedom**: jazzs improvisational style reminds us of the importance of creativity and freedom in the arts

## **hip hop in the 2020s**

### *context*
- **who**: modern hip hop artists include kendrick lamar, drake, and cardi b
- **what**: hip hop is a genre that combines rhythmic beats with rapping, and also includes djing, breakdancing, and graffiti
- **when**: hip hop began in the late 20th century and grew to be a major cultural force by the 2020s
- **where**: it started in the bronx, new york, but is now a global phenomenon
- **why**: hip hop grew from the need for marginalized communities to speak out about issues like poverty, violence, and injustice

### *important vocabulary and historical terms*
- **breakdancing**: a style of street dancing that was part of hip hop culture, created by african american and puerto rican youth in the 1970s
- **graffiti**: street art thats often connected to hip hop culture, where artists mark public spaces with their designs

### *impact on people and country*
- **fashion influence**: hip hop artists set trends in fashion, influencing how people dress today
- **political activism**: hip hop has become a platform for addressing issues like police brutality and racial inequality
- **global reach**: hip hop has spread worldwide, affecting musical styles and cultural expressions everywhere

### *lessons from the 1920s*
- **cultural expression as a catalyst for change**: like jazz in the 1920s, hip hop continues to challenge social norms and drive change in society
- **economic opportunities**: both jazz and hip hop have created economic opportunities in music, fashion, and media

## **similarities and differences**

### *similarities*
- **origins in marginalized communities**: both jazz and hip hop came from african american communities and served as forms of self-expression and resistance
- **innovative musical styles**: jazz introduced improvisation, while hip hop introduced sampling, both pushing the boundaries of music
- **cultural impact**: both genres have influenced fashion, language, and how people think about society

### *differences*
- **technological influence**: jazz spread through live performances and radio, while hip hop has used digital platforms and social media to spread its message
- **commercialization**: hip hop has become more commercialized, with artists expanding into industries like fashion, film, and technology
- **globalization**: jazz had a global influence, but hip hops globalization is even more widespread, influencing cultures across the world
